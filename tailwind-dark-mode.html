Support a dark color scheme

Dark-mode variants let you provide alternative utilities when a dark theme is active. The activation strategy depends on your Tailwind version and project configuration; it may follow the operating-system preference or a class controlled by your app.

Key idea

Design both themes intentionally. Check text contrast, borders, form controls, and focus rings in each mode rather than simply inverting colors.

Design both color schemes as one system

A dark-mode variant applies a style when the project's dark-mode condition is active. Depending on Tailwind version and configuration, that condition can follow the operating-system preference or a class/data attribute controlled by the application. Confirm which mechanism the project uses before adding variants.

Dark mode is not just a dark page background. Surfaces, borders, muted text, links, focus rings, and status colors may all need adjustment to preserve contrast and hierarchy. A light-mode shade that works on white can become too bright or lose distinction on a dark surface.

Test switching themes with every component state, including hover, keyboard focus, disabled controls, and error messages. If the application stores a manual preference, ensure it remains consistent across navigation and does not create a flash of the wrong theme during page load.

Example

Base utilities describe the light appearance, while dark: utilities provide a coordinated dark surface and readable text.

<article class="bg-white text-slate-900 dark:bg-slate-900 dark:text-slate-100">
  <h2 class="font-semibold">Reading preferences</h2>
  <p class="mt-2 text-slate-600 dark:text-slate-300">Choose a comfortable theme.</p>
</article>

Practice

Add a dark variant to a card and its border. Test both theme states and verify links and focus indicators remain visible.

Try the example, change one detail, and explain how the result changes.
