Audio and Video

Explore audio and video through a practical example and a focused exercise.

How it works

Native audio and video elements provide playback controls. Include captions for video dialogue and alternatives for media.

Build the mental model

The native audio and video elements let browsers expose familiar playback controls and multiple source choices. The controls attribute gives users a way to play, pause, adjust volume, and seek.

How to apply it

Captions communicate spoken dialogue and important sounds to people who cannot hear the track, while a transcript can make the information searchable and available in another format. Provide alternatives appropriate to the media and keep them synchronized with the content.

Common pitfalls

Autoplay can be disruptive and may be blocked by browsers, especially when sound is enabled. Do not make media the only way to receive essential information, and check that controls, captions, and fallback text work on the devices you support.

<video controls width="640" poster="preview.jpg">
  <source src="tour.mp4" type="video/mp4">
  <track kind="captions" src="tour-en.vtt" srclang="en" label="English">
  Your browser cannot play this video.
</video>

Play the example with sound off and confirm that captions or an equivalent transcript still communicate the important information.

Practice

Add a video with controls, a poster, and captions. Consider what alternative formats users may need.
