Artificial Intelligence Tutorial

Artificial intelligence (AI) is a broad field concerned with systems that perform tasks involving capabilities such as perception, reasoning, language, and decision-making. This course builds concepts before moving toward practical evaluation.

Learning goal: Explain how common AI approaches work, what they need, and how to assess their limitations responsibly. Follow the lessons in the sidebar; each topic has its own page with explanations, an example, and a practice task.

Course overview

Learn the relationship between AI and machine learning, how data and training shape model behavior, and how to evaluate predictions. Later lessons introduce neural networks, language systems, and responsible use.

Choose any topic from the expanded sidebar, or start with AI Fundamentals.

Build a map of the field

AI is the broad field; machine learning is one family of ways to build systems that learn from data, and generative AI describes systems that create content from learned patterns. Data representation, model training, and evaluation connect these ideas: the examples and objective shape what a model can do, while testing reveals where it falls short.

The lessons move from definitions and data to supervised and unsupervised learning, neural networks, and language and generative systems. They then bring the technical ideas back to practical judgment: choosing meaningful measures, recognizing bias and limitations, and protecting privacy.

For each approach, ask what evidence it uses, what kinds of errors it can make, and who is affected. AI is not automatically better than a clear rule or a human process. A sound explanation includes both a capability and the conditions under which it should not be trusted.

A first example

Use this small example as a starting point. The lessons explain the ideas behind it and how to adapt them to a real project.

task = "sort support requests by topic"
examples = ["billing question", "reset my password"]

# A simple baseline can be rules or a trained model.

How to practice

  1. Read one lesson and identify the key idea in your own words.
  2. Type or adapt its example, then predict what will happen before you run or inspect it.
  3. Complete the practice task and test at least one edge case.
  4. Review limitations and accessibility, privacy, or reliability concerns that apply.
