Use AI with appropriate safeguards

Responsible AI practice considers privacy, transparency, security, accessibility, fairness, and human oversight. The necessary safeguards depend on the data, users, purpose, and consequences.

Key idea

Minimize data collection, respect consent and retention rules, restrict access, and explain when AI contributes to a decision. Seek qualified review for regulated or high-impact uses.

Responsible use begins with purpose and boundaries

Before using AI, identify the purpose, who may be affected, what information is necessary, and what decision the system will influence. Collecting more data than needed increases privacy exposure without guaranteeing better results. Prefer approved, minimized inputs and explain the intended use clearly.

Privacy, fairness, accessibility, transparency, and human oversight are related design concerns. A user should know when AI is involved where that matters, understand meaningful limits, and have a route to correct important errors. A human reviewer needs enough context and authority to make an independent judgment.

Safeguards should be proportionate to impact: low-stakes assistance may need clear caveats and feedback, while consequential uses demand stronger validation, review, and governance. Revisit the risk when the data, model, or user population changes; responsible practice continues after launch.

Practice

For an AI feature, write a short notice explaining what it does, what data it uses, and how a user can get help or request correction.
