Embedding Content

Explore embedding content through a practical example and a focused exercise.

How it works

An iframe embeds another document, such as a map. Introduce embedded content clearly and give it a useful title.

Build the mental model

An iframe creates a browsing context containing another document, separated from the embedding page. A descriptive title tells users of assistive technology what that embedded region is for before they enter it.

How to apply it

Embedded services can bring their own controls, scripts, and tracking behavior, so include only content that benefits the page. Consider a lazy-loading strategy for off-screen embeds and set a deliberate size so the page does not jump as the content loads.

Common pitfalls

An iframe's content may be inaccessible, slow, or blocked by privacy settings; provide a direct link or useful fallback. Do not rely on an embedded document to supply the parent page's headings or landmarks.

<iframe src="https://www.openstreetmap.org/export/embed.html"
  title="Map of the city center" loading="lazy"
  width="600" height="400"></iframe>

Read the iframe's title without looking at its contents, then check that a direct alternative remains available if the embed fails to load.

Practice

Embed a map or video. Add a descriptive title and a nearby text link to the content.
