Work with human language

Natural language processing (NLP) covers methods for analyzing or generating human language. Tasks include text classification, search, translation, summarization, and extracting information.

Key idea

Text has ambiguity, context, dialect, and cultural variation. Measure quality across the kinds of language your users actually provide rather than relying only on clean examples.

Language is rich and context-dependent

Natural-language systems process text or speech, but people express the same intent in many ways. Tokenization breaks text into model-sized units; those units are not always whole words. Context, spelling variation, idioms, and ambiguity all influence how an input is interpreted.

Tasks such as classification, extraction, translation, and summarization have different success criteria. A sentiment classifier needs suitable labels and must account for context such as negation; an extractor needs to preserve which source text supports a field. Select evaluation examples that reflect the intended language and audience.

Language models may perform unevenly across dialects, languages, and writing styles. Avoid treating a confident output as a neutral reading of what someone said. Review errors with affected users in mind, and give people a way to correct or challenge consequential interpretations.

Example

Intent detection maps language to a task category. A safe product still confirms important changes and should not infer more than the message supports.

intent = detect_intent(message)

Practice
