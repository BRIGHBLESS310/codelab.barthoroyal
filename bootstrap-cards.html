Cards

Explore cards through a practical example and a focused exercise.

How it works

Cards group related content into flexible containers. Combine a body, title, text, and optional actions.

Build the mental model

A card groups related content into a self-contained block, often with a heading, body, image, and action. Its parts are optional; include only what helps a reader understand the information and its relationship to the rest of the page.

How to apply it

Cards can organize repeated items, but a card is not itself a semantic document role. Choose a suitable heading and meaningful link or button for its action, and keep image alternative text appropriate to the card's message.

Common pitfalls

Making the whole card clickable can obscure which action will occur and create nested interactive controls. Preserve clear focus targets, sensible heading order, and a responsive layout when cards wrap.

<article class="card h-100"><div class="card-body">
  <h2 class="card-title h5">Beginner workshop</h2>
  <p class="card-text">Learn the fundamentals in one afternoon.</p>
  <a href="workshop.html" class="card-link">View workshop details</a>
</div></article>

Identify the card's main subject, heading, and action, then check that each interactive element can be reached and understood independently.
