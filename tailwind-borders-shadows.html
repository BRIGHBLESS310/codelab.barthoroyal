Add structure with borders and shadows

Border, radius, divide, and shadow utilities help distinguish surfaces. Use them to support visual grouping, not as a substitute for clear spacing and hierarchy.

Key idea

A subtle border often communicates card boundaries more consistently than a heavy shadow. Keep decorative effects restrained so they do not compete with content.

Use edges and elevation to clarify structure

Border utilities set an element's edge, while shadow utilities suggest elevation relative to nearby content. A subtle border can define a card's boundary without relying on color alone; a restrained shadow can distinguish a popover from the page behind it.

These effects communicate hierarchy, so applying them to every element weakens their meaning. Pair them with spacing, headings, and grouping rather than using a shadow as the only signal that content is interactive. Check the treatment in both light and dark themes.

Border width, style, radius, and color are separate decisions. If a border appears missing, verify that a width or border utility is present and that the color contrasts with its background. Keep focus outlines distinct from decorative borders so keyboard navigation stays obvious.

Example

The thin border defines the card edge while a small shadow raises it slightly from the page. The rounded corners are applied consistently.

<article class="rounded-xl border border-slate-200 bg-white p-5 shadow-sm">
  <h2 class="font-semibold text-slate-900">Project status</h2>
  <p class="mt-2 text-slate-600">Three tasks are ready for review.</p>
</article>

Practice

Build two related cards. Use borders and spacing to show grouping, and compare the result with and without a shadow.
