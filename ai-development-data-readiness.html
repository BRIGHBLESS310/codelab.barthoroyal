Prepare and govern data

Data readiness includes relevance, quality, permissions, provenance, retention, and representation. For retrieval, organize trusted sources and keep documents current; for training, review labels and coverage.

Key idea

Collect the minimum information required, remove unnecessary identifiers, define access controls, and identify who updates or retires stale content.

Check fitness, rights, and lifecycle

Data readiness means more than having a file to train or prompt with. Check whether the information is relevant to the task, complete enough for the expected cases, consistently labeled where labels are needed, and representative of the people and situations in scope.

Trace provenance and permissions before use. Remove unnecessary identifiers, restrict access, and establish retention and deletion practices. Where the data is used as reference material, verify its authority, freshness, and version so that an answer can be grounded in approved content.

Data can encode historical gaps or harmful patterns even when it is technically clean. Review examples with domain expertise, document known limitations, and plan how updates will be governed. If the needed evidence cannot be collected or used appropriately, that is a reason to revise the design.

Example

The workflow begins from approved documents and preserves governance metadata. A stale or unauthorized source can undermine even a technically sound model.
